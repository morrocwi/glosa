## Q1 — Premature Category Stabilization (PCS): named cross-domain failure mode or domain-only content?

**n_sources: 7** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q1-001..007)

- **Same as glosa (agrees / structural convergence):** DiMaggio & Powell 1983
  (institutional isomorphism — q1-002) and Bowker & Star 1999 (boundary
  objects / classification stabilization — q1-004) both independently
  document a field or category moving from early diversity to locked-in,
  homogenized form, across many unrelated domains — direct structural support
  for treating PCS as a cross-domain kernel pattern, not content confined to
  its three originating case studies.
- **Different (narrower or orthogonal senses that must not be silently
  merged):** Al Essa et al. 2026 (q1-001) shows "premature closure" already
  has a well-defined, narrower, empirically-tested meaning in clinical
  cognitive-bias research — conflating it with PCS under one name risks
  borrowing the clinical term's authority for a broader construct it was
  never built to carry. The Lao PDR university-governance case (q1-005) is
  orthogonal: its "myth of institutional transplantation" shows *formal*
  closure decoupled from *substantive* reality, not *premature-timing*
  closure — a distinct failure mode a timing-only PCS definition would miss.
- **What would defeat an unqualified PCS claim:** "Can institutional theory
  be refuted, replaced or modified?" (q1-003) argues institutional-isomorphism
  theory is a "closed theory" whose domain can only be narrowed, not freely
  extended — naming PCS as a general kernel construct on the strength of
  three case studies, without an independent theoretical justification for
  why the same mechanism applies outside them, would be exactly the
  illegitimate domain-extension this source warns against. The ASEAN
  modular-adoption case (q1-006) would defeat an unqualified "template
  stabilization = failure" framing: ASEAN's own template selection plus
  consistent adaptation produced "substantial innovation and success,"
  showing category/template stabilization is not sufficient by itself for a
  red-flag verdict — absence of contextual fit/adaptation is the operative
  variable, not closure-timing alone.

## Q2 — Claim card as substitute for institutional certification: kernel rule or open agenda item?

**n_sources: 7** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q2-001..007)

- **Same as glosa:** Registered Reports (COS, q2-005) and F1000Research open
  peer review (q2-007) both structurally do the move Q2 proposes — replace an
  opaque institutional say-so with a public, mechanically checkable record —
  giving the claim-card idea a real structural precedent.
- **Different:** F1000's own "Approved" rating is still awarded by invited
  expert reviewers, not eliminated (q2-007) — it substitutes transparency for
  opacity, not expertise for no-expertise, a narrower and more defensible
  claim than "claim cards replace certification" stated without
  qualification. The TCI Group-1 accreditation guide (q2-006) shows what
  "institutional certification" concretely costs in this workspace's own Thai
  setting (multi-year peer review, regional citation-index stakes) — a
  reality check on what a certification-substitute clause would actually be
  substituting for.
- **What would defeat formalizing it:** Hardwicke et al. 2021 (q2-002) found
  16 of 25 open-data-badged articles (64%) had a major numerical discrepancy
  on re-check, and Brown 2024 (q2-001) documents open-science badges and
  Registered Reports being actively gamed for the appearance of rigour rather
  than its substance — a formally-complete certification-substitute
  credential does not by itself guarantee the checkable rigour it signals.
  Dudda et al. 2025's scoping review (q2-003) shows direct
  reproducibility-effect evidence for such interventions generally is thin
  (only 15 of many catalogued interventions had any direct outcome measured).
  Chakravorti et al. 2024 (q2-004) cautions any such clause would need
  regional/institutional adaptation (US vs. India professors differ
  significantly on reproducibility incentives), not travel as a universal
  rule. No source tested Q2's actual object (a Rule-17 claim card plus the
  I0–I5 ladder) directly — the honest reading is Q2 stays a logged `[Open]`
  agenda item pending a testable falsifier, not a formalized kernel rule.

## Q3 — Non-clinical/non-medical/non-legal AI scope-boundary clause: standardize or leave per-instance?

**n_sources: 8** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q3-001..008)

- **Same as glosa:** Addie & McNealy / Tech Policy Press (q3-007) reaches,
  independently and from a health-communication-equity angle, the same
  general-purpose-vs-health-product cut Q3 asks about — but frames it as a
  governance-process requirement (consultation, impact assessment,
  co-design), not disclaimer text.
- **Different:** EU AI Act Annex III (q3-001) and Thai FDA SaMD screening
  (q3-003) both classify by *function* (does the system gate benefits/is it a
  medical device) rather than by a portable disclaimer string — orthogonal to
  a text-level clause. The Thai DTAM AI guideline (q3-004) places a
  diagnostic example (chest-X-ray AI) and a non-diagnostic example
  (product-recommendation AI) under one undifferentiated technical category —
  evidence the clinical/non-clinical boundary is not yet salient in at least
  one real domestic AI-governance document.
- **What would defeat standardization:** FDA CDS FAQ (q3-002) shows the same
  software function can fall on either side of the device/non-device line
  depending on deployment context (time-critical or not) — a single reusable
  clause text cannot by itself carry that context-dependent classification.
  Sharma/Alaa/Daneshjou (q3-005) empirically shows medical-disclaimer
  presence in LLM/VLM outputs collapsed from ~20–26% (2022/2023) to ~1%
  (2025) even where disclaimer language and guidance have existed for years —
  standardizing clause text alone, without runtime enforcement, does not
  solve the underlying problem. Townsend et al. 2023 (q3-006, Global South,
  12 African countries) shows a reusable SaMD-style clause presupposes a
  legal category ("medical device" covering AI/algorithms) that is explicitly
  absent from the medical-device statutes of 9 of 12 countries studied — a
  concrete Global-South limit on "standardize across all our disclaimers."
  The Legal Paige commercial blog (q3-008) shows even a single non-clinical
  domain (creative freelance services) already needs service-specific,
  client-specific disclaimer variants — standardization fails to hold even
  within one unregulated domain.

## Q4 — Sources lacking an explicit claim-tier declaration: silent skip or flag-and-request-tier intake rule?

**n_sources: 8** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q4-001..008)

- **Same as glosa:** Shades of Grey (Adams et al., q4-006) and Khalil et al.
  2026 (q4-001) both document a real, measured gap — 81% of high-impact
  systematic reviews do not separately appraise grey literature, and "there
  is no universally accepted guidance" — supporting that *some* explicit
  intake handling is needed, not silent-skip.
- **Different:** GRADE (Balshem et al., q4-002) assigns evidence tiers to a
  *pooled body of evidence* the review team assembles, not to individual
  self-declaring sources — Q4's framing that sources "lack a declared tier"
  partly misdiagnoses whose job the tiering is. Cabrera & Pardo 2019 (q4-003,
  Latin America/Caribbean) shows a real 9,776-document pipeline already
  classifies undeclared-methodology sources into a residual bucket and
  proceeds, without pausing to request clarification from the source.
- **What would defeat a flag-and-request-tier rule:** AACODS (Tyndall 2010,
  q4-005) already names "a policy brief" as its worked example for
  undeclared/secondary grey material and answers "trace it to the original
  and judge accuracy yourself" — not "flag and request the source declare a
  tier." The WHO HEN Annex 4 guide (q4-008) routes all grey literature to one
  existing checklist (AACODS) rather than a distinct new step. Parkhurst &
  Abeysinghe 2016 (q4-007) argues that reducing evidence adequacy to
  "declared tier, yes/no" reproduces the exact category error the field is
  trying to move past (form vs. fitness-for-purpose). Uneke et al. 2020
  (ECOWAS/WAHO, q4-004, Global South) shows anecdotal, expert, and local
  knowledge are admitted policy evidence *by design*, not as gaps — a blanket
  flag-and-request-tier rule risks over-policing exactly the kind of source a
  Global South regional body considers legitimate.

## Q5 — Frozen preregistration before ethics, before data: hard gate rule or case-by-case?

**n_sources: 8** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q5-001..008)

- **Same as glosa:** COS Preregistration (q5-001) fixes a canonical
  data-collection freeze edge but is silent on ethics-timing — evidence for
  only half of Q5's compound rule. PACTR (Ndwandwe et al. 2022, q5-004,
  Pan-African registry) explicitly permits registering *before* ethics
  approval is granted (gated only on recruitment not having started) — a
  working precedent for the "before ethics" half.
- **Different:** Scientific Reports Registered Reports policy (q5-002) shows
  the flagship RR format requires ethics *already in place* at Stage 1
  submission, with formal registry deposit only *after* acceptance — the
  opposite sequencing from Q5's proposed "freeze strictly before ethics."
  TCTR Help Center (q5-006, Thailand) tracks a *third* clock — the registry's
  own administrative approval of the filing ("Released") — distinct from
  both protocol-freeze and the study's own IRB approval; a two-field schema
  would conflate two different events.
- **What would defeat a hard, universal gate:** Birajdar et al. 2019
  (q5-003, India CTRI) found 63% of 2016-registered studies were registered
  retrospectively (median lag 428 days) — a norm alone does not produce
  compliance; a timestamp field without enforcement is gameable. CASRAI
  (q5-007) warns explicitly against letting a passed sequencing check be read
  as ethics-*substance* verification — the two are categorically different
  processes. van Miltenburg et al. 2021 (q5-008, NLP preregistration) shows
  an entire live research field has no ethics-committee analogue for most of
  its work — a hard block on missing `ethics_approved_at` would incorrectly
  block legitimate non-human-subjects work; the schema needs a
  null/NOT_APPLICABLE state, not a bare before/after comparison.

## Q6 — Unverified-verifiability language ("verifiable" with no stated procedure): standing red flag?

**n_sources: 8** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q6-001..008)

- **Same as glosa:** NCSE "Weasel Words" (q6-003) gives a near-direct,
  plain-language restatement of Q6's exact pattern — a claim asserted
  "verifiable"/"debunked" with vague attribution and no stated checking
  mechanism. NOAA "Signs of a Predatory Publisher" (q6-006) shows an
  institution already running this exact red flag in production, one domain
  over (journal-quality evaluation: claimed indexing/impact metrics that
  turn out not verifiable).
- **Different:** Wikipedia "Words to watch" (q6-001) already names a
  *broader* existing pattern ("vague attribution" / weasel words) that Q6's
  proposal would be a narrower named sub-case of, not a wholly new pattern —
  argues for extending an existing disclaimer-catalogue entry rather than
  adding an unrelated one. Cofact (q6-005, Thailand) independently arrived at
  a *checkability* tag distinct from a *truth-value* tag, but answers "can WE
  check this claim," not "does the SOURCE claim checkability without showing
  how" — related but not identical to Q6's proposal.
- **What would defeat a purely mechanical schema flag:** Ott 2018 (q6-002)
  shows whether language is harmful "weasel wording" vs. legitimate
  epistemic hedging depends on the *author's intent to deceive*, which is
  explicitly not mechanically checkable from text alone — a keyword/pattern
  trigger risks flagging legitimate hedging as if it were deceptive. Jain &
  Singh (q6-007, India, predatory-publishing ecosystem) operates at a
  different unit of analysis (publisher-network scale) and was only read at
  abstract level — citing it as direct schema-design evidence would overstate
  what was read.

## Q7 — Infinity-as-transcendental-ground: rival stance to reconcile, or unreconciled tension to flag?

**n_sources: 8** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q7-001..008)

- **Same as glosa (i.e., support for "flag as open tension" over "formal
  reconciliation"):** Aristotle's actual/potential-infinity argument
  (q7-001) settles the mathematical debate on pure magnitude/coherence
  grounds with no transcendence category at all; SEP Intuitionism (q7-003)
  shows the *live, actively contested* rival stance in philosophy of
  mathematics today (constructivism/finitism vs. Platonism) proceeds with
  zero theological content; Wikipedia Finitism (q7-004) is itself a full
  rival epistemic stance on strictly mathematical grounds that glosa would
  need to reconcile with *before* reaching the transcendental-ground
  question — together arguing the mathematical dispute, not the theological
  one, is the nearer, more load-bearing rival to engage first.
- **Different:** Duns Scotus (q7-002) explicitly holds the theological and
  mathematical senses of "infinite" are *different senses of the word*, not
  one concept two traditions dispute — suggesting the honest framing may be
  two adjacent, non-competing uses of a term rather than a rivalry to
  reconcile.
- **What would defeat "flag and leave open" (i.e., push toward formal
  reconciliation):** Trlifajová (arXiv:2407.18972, q7-005) documents actual
  infinity as a theological question for a millennium, historically
  *settled* via theological argument (Vatican approval after neo-Thomist
  advocacy) before being accepted into mathematics — the transcendental-
  ground stance was load-bearing in getting actual infinity into mathematics
  at all, not an optional add-on. Apeiron/Anaximander (q7-006) is the
  earliest attested Western case of infinity-as-transcendental-ground,
  historically *prior* to Aristotle's distinction and to Cantor by
  centuries — cutting against treating it as peripheral. The Arabic
  popular-philosophy source on Plotinus (q7-007) and the Thai mgronline
  piece on Cantor/Hilbert (q7-008) show the transcendental framing has real
  cross-cultural, cross-language diffusion (specialist Neoplatonist
  apophatic reasoning and Thai popular-science retelling of "Cantor's
  paradise" alike) — modest but real evidence the stance is not fringe or
  purely rhetorical.

## Q8 — Ash'ari theological constructs (Dependent Order, Directed Selection, Dependent Identity; five registers of faqr): structural analogue for glosa's ontology, or domain-only?

**n_sources: 8** (cite-glosa-v0-3-q8-001..008)

- **Same as glosa (structural analogue exists, theology-neutral):** SEP
  "Ontological Dependence" (q8-002) gives a fully secular, mature formal
  apparatus (grounding theory, rigid/generic existential dependence,
  identity-dependence) matching and sharpening "Dependent Identity" with zero
  reference to God anywhere in its definitional machinery. SEP
  "Occasionalism" (q8-001) shows mainstream academic philosophy of religion
  already treats Ash'ari occasionalism and Western occasionalism
  (Malebranche, Berkeley) as instances of one general position. SEP
  "Causation in Arabic and Islamic Thought" (q8-004) shows al-Razi's
  "principle of preponderation" is one historical instance of the
  theology-independent Principle of Sufficient Reason (also Leibniz) — a
  secular formal home for "Directed Selection" already exists in general
  form.
- **Different:** Fawwaz Noor Amirul Huda 2020 (q8-006, Indonesia) shows a
  Global South/Southeast Asian Islamic-education tradition does *not* draw
  an ontology/theology line at all ("filsafat ketuhanan" = ontology) — the
  Western analytic sources maintain a separability Q8 assumes, but that
  separability is itself a borrowing-vocabulary property, not a property the
  source tradition already has.
- **What would defeat lifting the constructs theology-free:** al-Aqtisadiyah
  (q8-005, Arabic) shows kasb (Dependent Order's closest analogue) is
  proverbially "more hidden" than any other Ash'ari doctrine even *within*
  its own tradition — extracting a structure risks exporting an ambiguity,
  not a clean mechanism. The dergipark Ibn 'Arabi/Rumi paper (q8-007) frames
  faqr's ontological content and its devotional/theological function as one
  inseparable thing, not two layers. IEP "Process Philosophy" (q8-003) shows
  process philosophy's own most developed account of "Directed Selection"
  (concrescence) has to reintroduce a God-equivalent "principle of
  limitation" to do the actual selecting work — the analogue is relocated,
  not secularized. WorldAtNet (q8-008, devotional) shows faqr's structural
  content is expressed with "its Creator" built into the claim itself, not
  as removable decoration — a theology-neutral restatement would have to
  actively strip the relational term, a stronger move than "finding an
  analogue."
