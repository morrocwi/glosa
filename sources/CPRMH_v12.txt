In brief Problem. Existing plaque-regression literatures explain lipid lowering, efferocytosis, inflammation resolution, calcification and cap repair
largely in parallel, but do not specify when improvement in one material-handling route is compatible with safe remodeling of the others. Theory. CPRMH

Abstract
Atherosclerotic plaque is a multi-material, multi-cellular structure
containing retained apoB lipoproteins, intracellular and extracellular
cholesterol, apoptotic and necrotic material, extracellular matrix,
inflammatory cells and calcium-phosphate mineral in different phases.
These components are often studied through separate literatures. This
creates a theoretical gap: what combination of material-handling
processes would allow an established plaque to remodel while
preserving or improving structural stability?
This article develops the Coordinated Plaque Remodeling Modality Hypothesis (CPRMH) through a Theoretical Integrative Review
and Perspective Synthesis. CPRMH distinguishes six positive remodeling modalities: suppression of new mineral formation; spatially confined mineral resorption; molecular cholesterol export;
efferocytosis; active inflammation resolution; and structural stabilization/repair. It contrasts these with negative modalities such
as diffuse acidification, protease spillover, foam-cell export failure,
defective efferocytosis, mature-mineral persistence and excessive
apoB inflow, while allied modalities alter the conditions under which
positive remodeling can occur. The central proposition is that safe
regression is a coordination problem, not the maximization of any
single clearance pathway. Coordination is defined as prospectively
specified causal dependency among stage-specific load-bearing axes
and may be expressed through effect modification, serial mediation,
perturbational blockade, or convergent combinations of these signatures. A complementary Negative-Modality Lock prospectively
specifies which failure states are allowed to block the predicted
transition, preventing N1–N9 from becoming a post-hoc rescue layer.
A complementary Measurement-and-Threshold Lock pre-specifies
how target engagement and blocker control are measured and how
thresholds are adjudicated, preventing failed predictions from being
rescued by retrospective claims that an assay was inadequate or a
cutoff should have been moved.
The synthesis resolves several apparent contradictions. Protonmediated mineral dissolution is chemically plausible, but diffuse
plaque acidity can impair cholesterol efflux and amplify inflammation;

therefore the relevant variable is spatially confined, mineral-facing
proton activity rather than systemic or diffuse acidification. Lipid
removal is routed through molecular cholesterol export rather than
bulk dissolution. Efferocytosis and pro-resolving signaling reduce
secondary necrosis, but clearance is only favorable when structural
repair keeps pace. Likewise, increasing calcium density can accompany plaque stabilization, so total calcium loss is not treated as a
universal therapeutic endpoint.
A second proposition, the Sleep-Anchored Grassroots Daily
Remodeling Cycle (SAGDRC), asks whether recurrent low-cost
behaviors can alter the probability of entering favorable modalities.
Instead of fixed wall-clock times, SAGDRC anchors food withdrawal,
fasting, caffeine withdrawal and activity to the individual’s principal
sleep–wake episode, while retaining endogenous circadian phase
as a competing biological explanation. The revised framework
separates sleep-anchor stability from anchor position and includes a
renal–metabolic adapted branch for obesity, type 2 diabetes and CKD
G3, in which prolonged fasting is not mandatory and diet, activity
and monitoring are adjusted to renal and medication constraints.
Embedded movement and ordinary low-cost foods are treated as
first-line implementation resources rather than requiring protected
exercise time, supplements or specialty foods.
Recent preclinical evidence that intermittent fasting can regress
established Ldlr-deficient mouse plaque through a BMAL1–MERTK
efferocytosis axis strengthens one state-setting branch of the framework but does not establish the full CPRMH chain or human efficacy.
Accordingly, CPRMH specifies rival explanations, boundary conditions, seventeen falsification tests, a stage-specific pre-specification
stack for load-bearing axes, negative-blocker sets, measurements and
decision thresholds, and a staged empirical program. Safe-regression
adjudication further requires prospectively justified harm margins
for critical structural-safety endpoints rather than treating a nonsignificant safety test as evidence of no harm. Its intended contribution
is a testable coordination architecture that connects plaque material
routing, temporal physiology and health-equity constraints while pre-

2. The Analytical Problem: Mechanistic Knowledge
Without a Shared Remodeling Architecture
The literature already provides strong mechanistic accounts of
several components of atherosclerosis:
• VSMCs can undergo osteogenic transformation and promote
vascular calcification.
• Calcium-phosphate crystal growth is actively inhibited by
endogenous regulators such as pyrophosphate.
• Macrophages can both promote calcification and participate
in ectopic demineralization.
• Macrophages export cholesterol through ABCA1/ABCG1dependent pathways.
• Advanced plaques show defective efferocytosis and accumulation of necrotic material.
• Specialized pro-resolving mediators can improve efferocytosis and plaque stability in experimental models.
• Intensive lipid lowering can reduce non-calcified plaque
burden while increasing or densifying calcified components.
• BHB can inhibit vascular calcification in several preclinical
models.
• Fasting and time-restricted feeding can improve or worsen
atherosclerosis depending on metabolic and genetic context.
The problem is therefore not absence of mechanisms.
The problem is one of analytical organization.
Most literatures ask questions such as:
• Does BHB inhibit calcification?
• Does macrophage CA2 dissolve ectopic mineral?
• Does ABCA1 promote cholesterol efflux?
• Does MerTK-mediated efferocytosis reduce necrotic core?
• Do resolvins improve plaque stability?
• Does time-restricted feeding reduce atherosclerosis?
• Does higher calcium density indicate stabilization?
Each question is important, but the literatures rarely ask
what must happen simultaneously or sequentially if a plaque
is to lose hazardous material without becoming mechanically
unstable.

1. Introduction: From “Removing Plaque” to
Coordinating Plaque Remodeling
Atherosclerosis is not a single-material obstruction. An advanced plaque can contain retained apoB-containing lipoproteins, extracellular and intracellular cholesterol, cholesterol
crystals, apoptotic and necrotic cellular material, extracellular
matrix, vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), inflammatory
cells, and calcium-phosphate mineral in multiple structural
states [68, 70]. Consequently, the intuitive question—how can
plaque be cleaned?—is biologically underspecified.
Three common simplifications create problems.
First, plaque regression is often imagined as bulk material
dissolution. This is misleading for lipid. The physiologically
plausible route for removing excess macrophage cholesterol
is molecular export through transporters such as ABCA1
and ABCG1 to extracellular acceptors, followed by systemic
lipoprotein transport, rather than the release of macroscopic
lipid fragments into blood [12, 14, 70].
Second, vascular calcium is frequently treated as one
homogeneous substance. In reality, calcium-phosphate deposition is a dynamic process involving nucleation, amorphous
or poorly crystalline phases, crystal maturation, hydroxyapatite
accumulation, microcalcification, macrocalcification, and variable mineral density. The biological significance of calcium
also depends on location and morphology: microcalcification
in mechanically vulnerable regions is not equivalent to dense
macrocalcification within a more stable fibrocalcific plaque
[27, 29, 30, 68].
Third, acid-mediated demineralization is often conceptualized without spatial scale. Hydroxyapatite dissolution is
strongly pH-dependent, and osteoclasts use proton secretion to
resorb mineral. Yet acidic extracellular plaque fluid can impair
cholesterol efflux and amplify inflammation and proteolysis
[8, 9]. The biologically relevant distinction may therefore be
not whether acid is present, but whether proton activity is
confined to a sealed mineral-facing microcompartment or
diffuses across the plaque environment.
These observations suggest a different object of inquiry:

Coordinated plaque remodeling across mineral, lipid, cellulardebris, inflammatory, and structural compartments.

4. Nearest Neighbors and the Discriminating Novelty
of CPRMH

3.3 Clearance–repair coordination gap
Material removal alone is insufficient. If mineral or matrix
is removed faster than VSMCs can maintain collagen and the
fibrous cap, plaque vulnerability may increase. Efferocytosis,
pro-resolving signaling, and structural repair must therefore be
incorporated as co-requisites rather than downstream details.

The novelty claim is deliberately narrower than saying that
plaque regression, efferocytosis, calcification, meal timing
or fasting are individually new. Contemporary scientific
statements already describe regression as involving lower
lipid burden, reduced inflammation, improved efferocytosis,
cap thickening and changes in calcific phenotype [68, 69].
Reviews also connect macrophage phenotype, efferocytosis
and vascular calcification [6, 7, 70], while chrononutrition
literature increasingly links feeding time to cardiovascular and
circadian physiology [67].
The proposed advance is the analytical object: CPRMH
asks whether these processes form a constrained, materialspecific remodeling system in which the benefit of one route
depends on the state of the others. Table 2 shows the nearestneighbor challenge.

3.4 Stage-dependence gap
Early macrophages, advanced foam cells, calcified plaques,
diabetes-associated lesions, and CKD-associated mineralization are biologically different states. A pathway available in
one stage may be absent in another. For example, GPR109A
signaling is down-regulated in human macrophage-derived
foam cells, limiting extrapolation from receptor-mediated
anti-inflammatory or efflux mechanisms in less lipid-loaded
cells.
3.5 Everyday-state translation gap

Discriminating novelty. CPRMH earns theoretical complexity
only if a multi-modality coordination term explains safe plaque
remodeling beyond what can be explained by apoB reduction,
weight loss, glycemic improvement, circadian alignment, isolated
efferocytosis or formation suppression alone.

Fasting, meal timing, exercise, and diet are often treated as
interventions in their own right. Yet these practices may be
better conceptualized as state-setting exposures that alter
ketones, insulin, glucose, lipoprotein flux, inflammatory signaling, substrate availability, and circadian physiology. Their
relevance to plaque remodeling depends on whether they
actually shift the underlying cellular modalities.

3.6 Feasibility, time-cost, and health-equity gap
A lifestyle exposure can be biologically plausible yet socially
unusable. Conventional cardiovascular advice often assumes
discretionary time, stable work hours, access to safe exercise
space, separate meal-preparation time, and the ability to organize sleep around a daytime work schedule. These assumptions
exclude or burden shift workers, caregivers, informal workers, low-income households, and people whose work already
consumes most of their physical and temporal capacity.
The relevant translational object is therefore not merely
recommended behavior but feasible health-maintenance possibility: what can be incorporated into a person’s existing life
without requiring substantial additional money, travel, equipment, or protected leisure time. This distinction parallels the
difference between nominal opportunity and practically usable
opportunity developed in social analyses of sporting possibility:

depends less on maximizing any single clearance mechanism
than on coordinating material-specific clearance, suppression of

cellular overload.

6. Positive Modalities

Pre-specified negative modality = prediction. Post-hoc negative
modality = rescue. CPRMH permits the first and prohibits the
second for adjudicating the same dataset.

6.1 Positive Modality 1: Suppression of New Mineral
Formation
Vascular calcification is not only passive precipitation. VSMC
osteogenic transition, apoptosis, extracellular vesicles, inflammatory signals, phosphate load, and loss of endogenous
inhibitors all contribute to mineral formation. Hydroxyapatite
deposition therefore reflects both physicochemical permissiveness and active cell-regulated processes.
This distinction matters because stopping new mineral
formation is biologically easier than removing mature
mineral.
BHB has emerged as a candidate suppressor of vascular calcification in preclinical systems. Three partially independent
mechanistic lines are notable:
1. Autophagy enhancement. BHB reduced high-phosphateinduced arterial and VSMC calcification, and inhibition of
autophagy attenuated this protection.
2. HDAC9–NF-𝜅B modulation. BHB reduced VSMC and
arterial calcification in CKD-related models while downregulating HDAC9 and NF-𝜅B signaling.
3. AMPK/Nrf2 and ER-stress modulation. In ApoEdeficient mice and VSMCs, BHB reduced calcification
and ER-stress-mediated apoptosis.
These results support a formation-suppression modality.
They do not demonstrate mature mineral removal.

The framework distinguishes three classes.
Positive modalities. States or mechanisms that favor safe remodeling:
1. suppression of new mineral formation;
2. spatially confined mineral resorption;
3. molecular cholesterol export;
4. effective efferocytosis;
5. inflammation resolution;
6. fibrous-cap preservation or repair;
7. where appropriate, transformation toward a denser, more
stable calcific phenotype.
Negative modalities. States or mechanisms that block clearance
or increase structural risk:
1. diffuse extracellular acidification;
2. incomplete osteoclast-like differentiation;
3. protease spillover into the fibrous cap;
4. foam-cell failure of cholesterol-export machinery;
5. defective efferocytosis;
6. mature-mineral persistence;
7. excessive apoB influx or repeated hyperlipidemic exposure;
8. disease-state mismatch;
9. state-setting exposures that worsen rather than improve
downstream clearance.

Ketone-independent pharmacologic triangulation. The
formation-suppression arm is not dependent on BHB
alone. Dapagliflozin has been reported to attenuate VSMC
osteogenic transdifferentiation and vascular calcification
through an SGLT2–SIRT1 pathway with SIRT1-mediated
deacetylation and degradation of HIF-1𝛼 [47]. Canagliflozin
has independently reduced vascular calcification in CKD-rat,
vitamin-D-overload mouse, arterial-ring, and VSMC models
through suppression of the NLRP3–caspase-1–IL-1𝛽 axis,
with NLRP3 inhibition and knockdown providing mechanistic
controls [48]. These findings are important to CPRMH
because they show that a formation-suppression modality can
be entered through more than one upstream state-setting route.
They also prevent the theory from becoming a ketosis-only
explanation.

Allied modalities. Mechanisms that are not themselves clearance but improve the environment in which positive modalities
can operate:

2. pyrophosphate and MGP-related mineral-growth inhibition;
3. autophagy/lipophagy;
4. specialized pro-resolving mediator pathways;
5. restoration of hepatic lipoprotein clearance;

6.2 Positive Modality 2: Spatially Confined Mineral
Resorption
The chemical premise is straightforward: calcium-phosphate
mineral becomes more soluble as proton activity increases.
Bone resorption demonstrates a biological implementation
of this chemistry. Osteoclasts attach to mineralized surfaces,
create a sealing zone, acidify the resorption lacuna through
V-ATPase-dependent proton transport, and dissolve hydroxyapatite while the surrounding extracellular environment remains

a fixed fasting duration alone.

9.1 The Sleep-Anchored Grassroots Daily Remodeling
Cycle (SAGDRC)
SAGDRC is a translational sub-hypothesis of CPRMH. It
proposes that recurrent daily coordination of feeding, movement, stimulant withdrawal, fasting, and sleep may increase
the duration or frequency of remodeling-permissive states
without requiring expensive products, gym membership, or
standardized work schedules.
The cycle begins with a Level 0 temporal condition and
then adds four temporal gates and two movement levels. Level
0 is not a demand for a conventional daytime schedule. It is
the attempt to preserve the most stable feasible principal-sleep
or partial-anchor window within the person’s actual work and
caregiving constraints. The theory therefore separates stability
of the anchor from where the anchor sits on the clock.

9.2 Why sleep and wake are the primary practical anchors

as an active repair interval, not merely as dead time between
dietary interventions.

9.3 Application to night-shift and non-standard workers
A night-shift worker should not be instructed to stop starch
at 14:00 simply because a daytime worker does. Instead, the
same sequence is shifted around the worker’s principal sleep
episode.
If a worker intends to sleep at 09:00, for example, the
relative architecture places caffeine withdrawal roughly around
midnight to 01:00, the proposed starch-reduction gate roughly
around 03:00–05:00, and the last-calorie gate roughly around
06:00–07:00. Fasting then spans the main daytime sleep period.
These times are illustrations generated from the relative rules,
not universal prescriptions.
For rotating shifts, the framework no longer treats full
re-anchoring to each upcoming sleep episode as the preferred
default. Instead it uses a hierarchy: first preserve the most
stable feasible sleep or partial-anchor window; second
position relative gates around that anchor; third, when
work makes the anchor move, quantify the degree of displacement and circadian misalignment rather than silently
redefining them away. Full cycle-by-cycle recalculation remains a comparator strategy, not the assumed optimal strategy.
This revision creates a direct test of Negative Modality 9:
whether sleep-anchor stability adds biological value beyond
total sleep duration and total energy intake.

rotating schedules rather than a simple universal ranking [76].
SAGDRC therefore targets stability of the sleep opportunity

employment schedule is globally safer.

9.8 The novel temporal proposition
The new proposition is not that any one element—fasting,
walking, low-starch evening meals, caffeine withdrawal, or
sleep—independently “cleans” plaque.
It is that:
Repeated coordination of low-cost behaviors around the individual’s sleep–wake cycle may create recurrent remodelingpermissive windows in which pathological inflow and new
mineral formation fall while lipid handling, autophagic processes, efferocytosis, resolution, and structural repair become
relatively more favorable. Repetition of these windows may cumulatively shift plaque dynamics from accumulation-dominant
toward stabilization- and, under narrower permissive conditions, regression-compatible states.

This is a new synthesis claim. Its novelty lies in temporal
coordination, relative-time anchoring, material-specific
clearance, and feasibility, not in asserting that walking, sleep,
vegetables, or fasting were individually unknown before this
article.

10.3 Dose-translation and mediator problems
Physiological BHB exposure during common 12–16-hour
fasting is generally lower than the millimolar concentrations
used in several mechanistic experiments. Fasting duration
alone is therefore a poor exposure variable. Future work
should characterize BHB peak × duration × recurrence ×
tissue exposure while measuring energy intake, hepatic lipid
flux, VLDL secretion, insulin, glucose, fatty-acid flux, apoB
and circadian phase.
The new BMAL1–MERTK result also strengthens the mediator problem: a fasting effect need not be a ketone effect. BHB,
circadian transcription, caloric exposure, lipoprotein flux and
immune-state changes may operate in parallel or sequentially.
The 12 August 2026 cardiovascular chrononutrition review
likewise emphasizes circadian alignment as a major candidate
mechanism of timed feeding [67].

13. Pre-Specified Outcome Adjudication
A coordination theory becomes non-falsifiable if every mixed
result can be re-described as “remodeling.” CPRMH therefore
requires a study-level adjudication rule before outcomes are
inspected. Each empirical test must pre-specify (i) the target
population and causal contrast, (ii) one primary plaque-burden
or material endpoint appropriate to the study, (iii) a set of critical structural-vulnerability / safety endpoints, (iv) the analysis
window and treatment of intercurrent events, and (v) clinically
or biologically justified change and harm margins. This
follows the general logic of prospectively defining the target
estimand and interpretation before analysis rather than allowing
the observed data to redefine the question [79]. Biomarker
improvement alone cannot establish plaque regression.

rather than a universally fixed clock interval. The primary

sleep-relative timing, SAGDRC must be revised toward a

14.14 Falsifier F14: Measurement-Adequacy Lock Test
15.2 Mineral phase

A failed prediction cannot be rescued by declaring the locked
assay, imaging measure, or biomarker inadequate after it has
passed the pre-specified 𝑀𝑠 quality-control and adequacy rules.
If independent evidence demonstrates a true measurement
failure, the study is measurement-indeterminate and cannot
support or refute the affected edge. If no such failure is
demonstrated, the observed null, inversion, or exhaustion
result remains negative evidence for the architecture. Repeated
dependence on post-hoc measurement objections is evidence
that the programme is methodologically degenerating.

Amorphous or poorly crystalline calcium phosphate may be
more labile than mature hydroxyapatite. Mineral-phase composition should therefore be measured rather than inferred
from CAC alone.
15.3 Renal function
CKD changes phosphate handling, PPi balance, acid–base
physiology, VSMC calcification pressure, inflammation, medication exposure, and mineral metabolism. It is therefore a
modifier of modality availability and dose, not an exclusion
criterion. CKD evidence cannot be generalized automatically
to individuals with normal renal function, and evidence from
normal renal physiology cannot be assumed to transfer unchanged into CKD. CKD G3 should additionally be stratified by
eGFR, albuminuria, laboratory phenotype, nutritional status,
and medication context rather than treated as one homogeneous
state [71, 72].

14.15 Falsifier F15: Threshold-Robustness Test
Target-engagement, blocker, and safety conclusions must be
tested across the pre-specified plausible 𝑇𝑠 range. If CPRMH is
supported only at a narrow cutoff selected because it preserves
the predicted architecture, while nearby biologically plausible
thresholds reverse the adjudication, the evidence is thresholdfragile and cannot count as strong confirmation. Repeated
threshold fragility across independent datasets weakens the
corresponding modality or edge.

15.4 Diabetes, obesity, and metabolic dysfunction
Diabetes and obesity can alter macrophage phenotype, efferocytosis, oxidative stress, lipid handling, glycemic exposure,
sleep, physical capacity, and vascular calcification. These
changes can make some positive modalities harder to enter
while simultaneously increasing the potential value of lowering
pathological inflow. The framework therefore includes an
explicit metabolic–renal adapted branch rather than assuming
that a positive modality observed under normal metabolism is
either automatically available or automatically unavailable in
diabetes. Weight management, physical activity, and dietary
quality remain legitimate allied modalities in people with
diabetes, obesity, and CKD when individualized to renal and
medication constraints [71, 72, 73].

14.16 Falsifier F16: Causal-Precedence / Non-Circularity
Test
A blocker or mediator must be operationally distinct from the
downstream outcome it is invoked to explain. If a negative
modality can be identified only by observing the very plaque
failure that it is supposed to cause, the explanation is circular
and inadmissible for causal adjudication. Repeated inability
to measure the proposed blocker upstream or independently
means that the blocker cannot remain a load-bearing explanatory element of the prospective architecture.
14.17 Falsifier F17: Minimum-Core Collapse Test
The minimum CPRMH core is rejected for a pre-specified
established / advanced-plaque domain when at least two
independent or orthogonal causal tests converge on one of
two patterns under adequate 𝑀𝑠 , robust 𝑇𝑠 , locked safety adjudication, and no post-hoc rescue: (a) a functionally sufficient
single-axis model produces safe remodeling while candidate
co-requisite axes add no necessary causal information; or (b)
the locked multi-axis architecture achieves target engagement,
the relevant blockers are exhausted, and the locked causal
signatures are observed, yet the predicted stabilization / saferegression phenotype still fails and a simpler rival model
explains the result better. Under either replicated pattern, the
coordination claim for that declared core domain is falsified,
not merely weakened. A new theory may be constructed, but
it is not confirmation of the failed CPRMH core.

16. Methodology
16.1 Research design

A behavior that requires extra travel, equipment, childcare,
meal-preparation time, or protected leisure may fail even
when biologically effective. Feasibility is therefore part of
exposure validity, not an afterthought. The model predicts
higher population reach when safe movement is embedded into
transport, household activity, caregiving, and other ordinary
routines before additional exercise is prescribed.
Occupational physical activity is treated separately from
freely chosen movement. A worker who already performs
prolonged manual labor may not benefit from adding more
work-like activity. Repetitive lifting, static loading, prolonged
standing, heat exposure, insufficient recovery, and low control
over intensity can create a different cardiovascular and musculoskeletal exposure from self-paced exercise. The model
therefore predicts that movement substitution, pacing, microrecovery, and added leisure activity where feasible may be
more relevant than simply increasing occupational workload. This is an equity safeguard against converting labor
burden into a health prescription.
15.9 Sex, age, genetics, and Lp(a)
Plaque phenotype, calcification burden, and lipoprotein biology vary by sex, age, and genetic background. ENPP1 and
ABCC6 provide causal human anchors for PPi-deficiencydriven ectopic mineralization, while PCSK9 loss-of-function
biology provides a human natural experiment for lifelong low
LDL exposure. Lp(a) is a major genetically influenced lipoprotein exposure associated with calcific and atherosclerotic
phenotypes and should not be assumed to be corrected by
fasting, exercise, or ordinary dietary timing. Sex and age can
modify plaque composition, calcification pattern, metabolic
response, and feasibility of lifestyle exposures. These variables
should therefore be pre-specified effect modifiers, not residual
covariates added after analysis.

16.7 Evidence discipline
Evidence types are not flattened.

Every load-bearing proposition must encounter:
• a near-neighbor mechanism;
• a rival explanation;
• contradictory evidence;
• a dose-translation problem where relevant;
• a human–animal translation challenge;
• a disease-stage boundary;
• a measurement limitation.
Contradictory findings are used to specify boundary conditions, not treated as noise.
Examples include:
• BHB suppresses vascular calcification, but physiological
fasting exposure may be lower than experimental doses;
• fasting reduces atherosclerosis in some LDLR models but
not ApoE models;
• local acidification can dissolve mineral, while diffuse acidity
impairs cholesterol efflux;
• calcification can increase during statin-mediated plaque
stabilization;
• macrophages express CA2 but may lack complete osteoclastlike resorptive capacity.

Layer B — cell/tissue mechanistic evidence. Can cells execute
the proposed process?
Layer C — animal evidence. Can the integrated organism
support it?
Layer D — human mechanistic/plaque evidence. Does the
machinery exist in human disease?
Layer E — human intervention/imaging evidence. Does modifying the system alter plaque composition or outcome?
Layer F — everyday-exposure evidence. Do fasting, exercise,
or diet generate the required physiological exposure in real
life?
No lower layer is automatically promoted to a clinical claim.

16.8 Limitations of the synthesis
This is a theory-led integrative synthesis rather than a systematic review or meta-analysis. The corpus is selectively
constructed for mechanistic leverage and therefore remains
vulnerable to literature-selection bias, single-author interpretation, uneven evidence density across domains, and differential
maturity of the component sciences. No claim of saturation is
made.
Several parameters are deliberately hypothesis-generating
rather than established guidance. The proposed 𝑆−4 to
𝑆−6-hour starch-reduction gate is not a validated clinical
threshold. Sleep-relative timing is a pragmatic field anchor
rather than a direct measure of endogenous circadian phase, and
experimental night-work studies indicate that biological-night
eating can remain harmful even when sleep timing is shifted.
Evidence among real-world shift workers remains sparse: a
2026 systematic review located only four randomized trials of
meal-timing interventions in shift-work settings [41].

20. Where the Framework Is Most Likely to Fail
The model should be explicitly weakened if any of the following
become well established:
1. Localized acidification cannot be achieved at vascular
mineral interfaces without diffuse plaque acidification.
2. Human plaque macrophages lack meaningful mineralresorptive potential under all physiologically attainable
states.
3. Advanced foam cells cannot recover cholesterol-export
capacity, making coupled clearance impossible without
pharmacological reprogramming.
4. Efferocytosis cannot keep pace with material mobilization in advanced plaque.
5. Everyday fasting/exercise exposures are too small or
transient to alter relevant plaque-cell states.
6. Observed clinical stabilization is explained fully by apoB
reduction and not by the proposed coordinated modalities.

Benefits attributed to sleep-anchored timing may actually be

Test: compare sleep-relative timing against dim-light melatonin onset or other circadian-phase markers in controlled

21. Claim Architecture: What Is Known, What Is
Synthesized, and What Is New
To protect novelty without overclaiming, CPRMH separates
four epistemic levels.
The absence of direct evidence for the full CPRMH/
SAGDRC chain does not reduce it to an invalid claim; it
identifies it as a hypothesis. The appropriate scientific response is therefore not to collapse the proposition back into
already-known lifestyle advice, but to make each novel relation
observable, falsifiable, and separable from simpler rivals.

23. Predictions That Require the Integrated Theory
The framework is only theoretically useful if it generates
expectations that do not follow from its component literatures
in isolation.
1. Parallel-dependency prediction. The benefit of enhancing efferocytosis will be smaller when pathological apoB
inflow remains high or when cholesterol export is severely
impaired, even if apoptotic-cell uptake itself improves.
2. Safety-coupling prediction. Increasing mineral-facing
resorption will not constitute favorable remodeling when
matrix proteolysis or cap thinning increases in parallel.
3. Asymmetry prediction. Interventions can reduce new
mineral formation and improve plaque stability without
materially removing mature apatite; therefore formation
and regression endpoints will frequently diverge.
4. State-setting interaction prediction. Fasting or exercise
effects will vary by plaque stage, apoB/hepatic-clearance
context and circadian alignment, rather than being uniformly
beneficial at a fixed duration.
5. Architecture-over-quantity prediction. A plaque can
become safer while calcium volume remains stable or
density rises, provided lipid/necrotic burden falls and cap
integrity improves.
6. Feasibility prediction. In population translation, an embedded low-cost protocol may generate greater cumulative
biological exposure than a more potent laboratory regimen
if the latter imposes large time, travel or financial costs; this
difference should disappear under experimentally matched
adherence.

24. From Mechanism to a Low-Cost
Health-Maintenance Architecture
The practical implication of CPRMH is not that individuals
should attempt to chemically dissolve plaque through food or
fasting. It is that ordinary life can be studied as a recurrent
vascular state-setting environment.
The grassroots extension therefore applies four design principles.
24.1 Embedded Activity First
Movement should first be recovered from activities people
already perform. A person who walks to transport, cleans a
home, carries goods, works standing, climbs stairs, or moves
through caregiving is not “doing nothing” simply because
that movement is not branded as exercise. Planned exercise
remains valuable, but it is a second layer.
This matters because the opportunity to exercise is socially
distributed. Time poverty, shift work, care obligations, unsafe environments, and transportation can make a 30-minute
dedicated exercise session materially more expensive for
one person than another; this extends the author’s earlier
opportunity-structure account of unequal sporting possibility
into preventive-health design [45]. A low-cost cardiovascular
framework should therefore count incremental time cost
alongside biological efficacy.
24.2 Relative Timing Instead of Middle-Class Clock Time
A universal instruction such as “stop eating at 18:00” or
“stop starch at 14:00” silently assumes a particular workday.
SAGDRC instead asks four personal questions:
• When is your principal sleep episode?
• When do you usually wake from it?
• How many hours before sleep can caffeine reasonably stop?
• How far before sleep can starch and then calories be progressively withdrawn without making the routine socially
or nutritionally unsustainable?
This allows the same theory to be used by a daytime office
worker, market vendor, factory worker, nurse, driver, caregiver,
or night-shift worker.
24.3 Ordinary Thai Food Before Health Products
The food architecture is deliberately compatible with low-cost
local diets. Its central resources are vegetables, legumes,
tofu, eggs, local fish, ordinary rice with less-refined grain
substitution where feasible, seasonal fruit, and water. Its
primary removals are repeated sugar-sweetened beverages,
heavily processed snacks and meats, excessive sodium, and
repeated starch-heavy eating near the main sleep episode.
This approach is aligned with contemporary cardiovascular
dietary guidance [35, 43, 44] but adds a different theoretical

24.4 Reduce Dependence by Reducing Preventable Load,
Not by Rejecting Care
The public-health ambition is to reduce the amount of avoidable
cardiometabolic disease that reaches expensive clinical stages.
It is not to imply that plaque disease, diabetes, hypertension,
acute coronary syndromes, or high-risk lipid disorders should
be self-treated without medical evaluation.
The strongest equity claim is therefore structural:
If the theory is correct, some proportion of cardiovascular
risk modification may be achievable by reorganizing time,
movement, eating sequence, sleep, and ordinary local foods—
resources already present in daily life—rather than by making
prevention depend first on commercial products, specialized
facilities, or high-cost programs.

This claim is empirically testable through adherence, cost,
biomarker, and eventually imaging outcomes.

A person wakes at 𝑊. Early in the waking period, substantial
meals including starch can occur, preferably with vegetables
and protein. Movement is accumulated through transport,
work, household tasks, and short post-meal activity. Around

𝑆−4 to 𝑆−6-hour range, starch-dominant foods are reduced.
Around 𝑆−2 to 𝑆−3 hours, caloric intake ends when feasible.
Fasting continues through the main sleep episode and may
extend briefly after waking before refeeding.
The exact wall-clock times change with the person’s life.
The sequence does not.

25. Conclusion
Atherosclerotic plaque remodeling is unlikely to be safely
achieved by a single “cleaning” mechanism. The literature
instead supports a distributed architecture in which different
plaque materials require different handling pathways and in
which the safety of clearance depends on coordination.
The strongest current evidence supports several separate
propositions: vascular mineral formation can be biologically
suppressed; macrophages can generate localized acidic extracellular compartments and can demineralize ectopic calcium under some conditions; macrophages export cholesterol
through molecular efflux pathways; defective efferocytosis
promotes necrotic-core expansion; pro-resolving mediators
can improve efferocytosis and fibrous-cap phenotype; and clinical plaque stabilization can occur while calcification density
increases.
These findings also impose strong constraints. Diffuse
plaque acidity is potentially pro-atherogenic. Human plaque
macrophages may have incomplete osteoclast-like resorptive
programs. Advanced foam cells can lose cholesterol-export
responsiveness. Mature hydroxyapatite can persist. Increased
resorptive protease activity could threaten the fibrous cap.
Fasting and BHB effects are context dependent and cannot be
equated with direct plaque clearance.

Appendix A. Claim-Discipline Rules for Future
Revisions
1. “Reduces calcification” must specify the endpoint.
Calcium deposition in cells, arterial calcium content, CAC progression, calcium density, and established mineral regression are
not interchangeable.
2. “Fasting effect” must specify achieved physiology.
Record BHB, energy intake, apoB, glycemia, weight change, and
timing.
3. “Acid-mediated clearance” must specify spatial scale.
Systemic acidification, diffuse extracellular plaque acidity, and
sealed mineral-facing acidification are distinct states.
4. “Macrophage clearance” must specify material.
Mineral resorption, lipid digestion, cholesterol efflux, and efferocytosis are distinct functions.
5. “Improved plaque” must specify architecture.
Include at least plaque volume, lipid/necrotic burden, calcific volume/density, inflammatory state, and cap stability where possible.
6. Preclinical evidence must remain preclinical.
No cell or animal mechanism is to be rewritten as a dietary or
fasting recommendation without direct human evidence.

7. Relative-time parameters must be labelled by status.
The caffeine cutoff is evidence-informed; the 4–6-hour starchreduction gate is a CPRMH/SAGDRC hypothesis parameter; the
2–3-hour last-calorie gate is a pragmatic starting rule. These must
not be presented as equally established facts.
8. Feasibility is part of causal translation.
A regimen that works only when participants possess abundant
free time, money, transport, or equipment cannot be generalized as
a low-cost population strategy without measuring those resources.
9. Shift work requires three-level interpretation.
Sleep-relative timing is a practical anchor, anchor stability is
a distinct exposure, and endogenous circadian phase remains a
biological modifier and rival explanation.
10. CKD / diabetes adaptation must not become exclusion.
Obesity, type 2 diabetes, and CKD G3 remain inside the translational model, but fasting duration, protein, sodium / potassium / phosphorus decisions, medication safety, hydration, and
monitoring must be individualized rather than copied from the
metabolically healthy branch.
11. A negative modality must be prospectively admissible.
For a given stage / contrast, specify the relevant 𝐵𝑠 , operational
marker, threshold or decision rule, expected direction, temporal
window and role before outcome access. An unspecified N1–N9
cannot be added later to reinterpret a null or harmful result as
support.
12. A new blocker belongs to the next theory version.
Discovery of an unmodeled blocker is scientifically valuable but
hypothesis-generating. It cannot adjudicate the dataset in which
it was discovered; prospective replication is required before the
revised blocker set can support CPRMH.
13. Measurement adequacy must be locked before outcome access.
Name the construct, assay / imaging method, QC limits, repeatability or precision rule, spatial / temporal scale, missingness rule
and adequacy floor. A method that passes these rules cannot be
disowned after the theory loses without independent evidence of
assay failure.
14. Decision thresholds must survive a plausible-range sensitivity
test.
Target-engagement, blocker and harm thresholds require a prespecified rationale and plausible range. A conclusion that survives
only at a narrow data-fitted cutoff is threshold-fragile rather than
confirmatory.
15. A blocker cannot be its own outcome.
Negative modalities and mediators must be measured upstream,
earlier, or through constructs distinct from the downstream endpoint they are invoked to explain.
16. The CPRMH core has a stopping rule.
In the declared established / advanced-plaque domain, replicated
orthogonal evidence for functional single-axis sufficiency or exhaustion failure under adequate measurement and robust thresholds
falsifies the minimum coordination core; it may motivate a new
theory but cannot be counted as confirmation of CPRMH.

Automated retrieval should be deliberately conservative. A machine flag is not equivalent to a harmful finding; it is an item requiring
human reading. Conversely, human adjudication should never silently
delete genuine discordance. The ledger therefore operationalizes the
article’s epistemic rule: contradiction defines boundary conditions
until evidence justifies stronger revision.

Example 4 — Obesity + type 2 diabetes + CKD G3. This example
